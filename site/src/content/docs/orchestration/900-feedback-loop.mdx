## What this skill is for

`900-feedback-loop` runs a two-role convergence loop around work that already happened: a Step 1 agent applies fixes, a Step 2 agent reviews the result against explicit criteria, and the loop repeats until Step 2 reports clean or a safety cap is hit. It does not perform the original work itself — the caller supplies the review criteria and how to apply fixes.

**Output**: No new `.specflow/` artifact of its own. Updates whatever files the Step 1 brief scopes in on later iterations, and returns a convergence report in chat.

## When to use it

- When another workflow needs a reusable review-and-converge step around a specific artifact or change set — this is how `901-feature-loop` checks every design/spec artifact and cleanup pass
- To review something and keep fixing it until it passes explicit criteria, without doing the apply-fix/review cycling by hand
- Whenever a Step 1 apply-fix brief and a Step 2 review brief can both be stated concretely

## When NOT to use it

- When the original creation work has not happened yet — this skill only wraps around work that already exists
- When review criteria cannot be stated concretely — a vague brief produces a vague, unreliable loop
- For one-off review with no intent to iterate — a direct `@reviewer` call is simpler

<Callout tone="core" title="Step 2 is the sole authority on convergence">
  This skill never overrides a `CHANGES_REQUIRED` verdict just because iterating further is expensive or slow. If the cap is hit, it escalates with the full iteration history instead of force-accepting the current state.
</Callout>

## What it produces

- A convergence report: total iterations run, what changed across them, and the final verdict
- On cap escalation instead: the iteration history, what feedback recurred, and the final outstanding items — handed back to the caller rather than silently given up on

## Required inputs

- **Step 1 brief** — the apply-fix objective, exact files/artifacts in scope, and how to apply review feedback. If the real creation work already happened outside this skill, say so explicitly; the first pass is then a no-op until Step 2 finds something.
- **Step 2 brief** — the exact artifact/scope to review and the concrete criteria to judge it against
- **Agent for each step** *(optional)* — defaults to `general-purpose` for Step 1 and `@reviewer` for Step 2
- **Iteration cap** *(optional)* — defaults to 5 apply-fix/review pairs

## Practical guidance

- Step 1 and Step 2 always run in separate, freshly spawned subagent contexts — never reuse one subagent's context for the other role, and never reuse a subagent across iterations
- Keep the loop's own working state small: the two briefs, the iteration count, and the latest verdict — do not accumulate subagent transcripts turn over turn
- Every iteration's Step 1 brief should be explicit about what it is doing, even a no-op pass

## Common mistakes

- Handing Step 1 a vague brief on later iterations instead of the exact feedback items Step 2 just returned
- Letting the loop run past the iteration cap by silently resetting the counter instead of escalating
- Reusing one subagent context across both roles, which lets the "reviewer" see its own fix reasoning instead of judging fresh

<Callout tone="tip" title="Default agents can be overridden">
  Step 1 defaults to `general-purpose` and Step 2 defaults to `@reviewer`, but the caller may name a different agent for either role — for example `@coder` for Step 1 when the fix is a real code change.
</Callout>
